What is a sales receipt and what is it for?

It is the proof you hand to a customer when you sell a product or service. For them it shows what they bought and how much they paid; for you it is a record of your sales and a safeguard against returns, warranties or claims. It is widely used by small shops, entrepreneurs, social media sellers and home-based businesses.

It is a simple proof of sale. It is not an electronic invoice and does not replace one: if your business is required to invoice, you must issue the invoice through your country's official system. If in doubt, consult an accountant.

What a sales receipt should include

  • Your business: name, contact details and address, with your logo.
  • The customer: name and, if needed, ID number or contact details.
  • Date and receipt number: with sequential numbering.
  • Sale details: a list of products or services with quantity and unit price.
  • Subtotal, discount and taxes: only if they apply.
  • Total due: clear and visible.
  • QR code: linking to your Instagram, WhatsApp or payment link, so customers can find you again.

How the total is calculated: discount and tax

The usual order is: add up each item (quantity × unit price) to get the subtotal; if you give a discount, it is subtracted from the subtotal; tax, if it applies, is calculated on the discounted amount; and the result is the total due. The generator does this math for you. An example:

  • Subtotal: $200,000
  • 10% discount: −$20,000
  • Taxable base: $180,000
  • 19% tax: $34,200
  • Total due: $214,200

The tax rate depends on your country and on what you sell; 19% is only an example.

Common mistakes on a sales receipt

  • Forgetting the receipt number or repeating it.
  • Not itemizing the products and showing only a total.
  • Applying the discount after the tax, which changes the total.
  • Giving a receipt without a date or without the business's contact details.
